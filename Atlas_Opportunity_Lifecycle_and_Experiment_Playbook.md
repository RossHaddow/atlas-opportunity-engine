# Atlas Opportunity Lifecycle & Experiment Playbook

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Approved planning specification  
**Purpose:** Establish consistent rules for researching, testing, operating, scaling, pausing, and killing opportunities in Project Atlas.

## 1. Lifecycle

Atlas uses the following opportunity statuses:

1. **Researching** — The opportunity is being investigated and validated.
2. **Testing** — A structured 30-day market experiment is underway.
3. **Active** — The test earned a cash profit and the opportunity is operating normally.
4. **Scaled** — The opportunity has proven consistent, efficient, repeatable, and capable of growth.
5. **Paused** — The opportunity remains valid but is temporarily inactive.
6. **Killed** — Work has stopped because the opportunity no longer justifies further resources.

## 2. Researching to Testing

An opportunity may move from **Researching** to **Testing** only when all three requirements are met:

- Evidence of customer demand
- A specific product or offer
- A written 30-day test plan

### Acceptable evidence of customer demand

One or more of the following may establish demand:

- Competitors making sales
- Search or marketplace activity
- Direct customer requests
- Survey or interview interest
- Successful comparable products

Atlas must record the underlying evidence. A simple unsupported statement that demand exists is insufficient.

## 3. Standard Testing Rules

### Default limits

- **Maximum initial cash budget:** $25
- **Standard test period:** 30 days
- **Progress checkpoints:** Weekly
- **Labor value:** $25 per hour

Spending above $25 requires Ross's approval before the expense is incurred.

### Required experiment record

Every 30-day test must document:

- What is being tested
- Product or offer
- Sales channel
- Tasks and launch date
- Approved budget
- Hours worked
- Revenue and expenses
- Customer response
- Final decision

### Weekly checkpoint

Each weekly review records:

- Work completed
- Hours invested
- Money spent
- Customer response
- Revenue earned
- Problems discovered
- Whether continuing the test still makes sense

Atlas may recommend early termination when evidence shows that a test is clearly failing. Atlas does not terminate an experiment automatically; Ross makes the final decision.

## 4. Financial Measurement

Atlas tracks two separate financial results:

### Cash profit

**Cash Profit = Revenue - Direct Expenses**

Cash profit determines whether the experiment technically passes its initial test.

### Labor-adjusted profit

**Labor-Adjusted Profit = Cash Profit - (Hours Worked x $25)**

Labor-adjusted profit determines whether the opportunity is genuinely worth the time invested and is required for Scaled status.

## 5. Testing to Active

An opportunity normally moves from **Testing** to **Active** only when it earns a cash profit within the 30-day test.

Revenue without profit is insufficient to pass automatically.

At day 30, Atlas must recommend one of the following:

- Move to Active
- Revise and retest
- Pause
- Kill

No experiment may remain indefinitely in Testing without a documented decision.

## 6. Failed Test Review

A test that does not earn a cash profit receives a case-by-case review rather than an automatic extension or automatic kill.

### Valid reasons to revise and retest

- Strong customer interest
- A clearly fixable problem
- Very little additional cost
- Strong long-term potential

Some revenue without profitability is not, by itself, sufficient justification for another test.

Two failed 30-day tests normally trigger a **Kill** recommendation. Ross may override that recommendation, but the reason for another attempt must be documented.

## 7. Active Review

An opportunity remains **Active** for 60 days before a mandatory decision review.

At the 60-day review, Atlas recommends:

- Scale
- Maintain as Active
- Revise
- Pause
- Kill

## 8. Requirements for Scaled Status

An opportunity must meet every requirement below before being marked **Scaled**:

- Two consecutive profitable months
- Positive labor-adjusted profit using the $25 hourly labor value
- A repeatable operating process
- A clear path to higher income
- Low ongoing maintenance

For money-faucet opportunities, **low ongoing maintenance** means no more than four hours of work per month.

An opportunity requiring more than four hours per month does not automatically fail, but it is no longer classified as low-maintenance and must be compared with other opportunities using its labor-adjusted return.

## 9. Scaled Review

Every Scaled opportunity receives a **monthly review** covering:

- Cash profit
- Labor-adjusted profit
- Hours required
- Maintenance burden
- Growth or decline
- Operational problems
- Continued strategic value

Atlas then recommends continuing, optimizing, reducing investment, pausing, or killing the opportunity.

## 10. Paused Status

Paused may be entered from Researching, Testing, or Active when an opportunity remains valid but temporarily cannot proceed.

Every Paused opportunity must be reviewed after **30 days**. At that review, Atlas recommends:

- Resume
- Remain Paused with a documented reason and new review date
- Revise
- Kill

## 11. Kill Rules

Atlas recommends **Killed** status when:

- There is no meaningful customer interest.
- The opportunity has failed two tests.
- The workload is too high for the return.

Cost overruns and competition from stronger opportunities require review but do not automatically kill an opportunity under this version of the playbook.

## 12. Decision Authority

Atlas gathers evidence, calculates performance, identifies rule violations, and makes recommendations. Ross retains final authority over:

- Approving spending above the $25 initial-test limit
- Ending a test early
- Extending or repeating a failed test
- Moving an opportunity between statuses
- Scaling or killing an opportunity

## 13. Decision Summary

| Decision | Rule |
|---|---|
| Initial test budget | Maximum $25 |
| Initial test period | 30 days |
| Test checkpoints | Weekly |
| Passing financial result | Positive cash profit |
| Labor valuation | $25 per hour |
| Active review | After 60 days |
| Consistent profitability | Two consecutive profitable months |
| Low-maintenance threshold | Four hours or less per month |
| Repeated failure | Two failed tests |
| Paused review | After 30 days |
| Scaled review | Monthly |
